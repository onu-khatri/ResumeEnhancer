# Evidence Synthesis

Use this reference when research spans multiple files, sources, participants, or observations. Synthesis should expose both corroboration and conflict, then connect evidence to a decision without turning inference into fact.

## Process

1. Review the source material without prematurely judging it.
2. Extract atomic observations: one fact, behavior, quote, metric, or repository condition per item.
3. Group related observations and name the resulting themes.
4. Compare themes across source types, segments, and timeframes; retain contradictions rather than averaging them away.
5. Write an insight as `observation + pattern + implication`, then state the confidence and decision impact.

Use affinity grouping for exploratory qualitative material. Use a simple coding progression of descriptive observation, interpretation, and cross-source theme when rigor or an audit trail matters. Use Jobs-to-be-Done framing only when the decision depends on the user's situation, motivation, and expected outcome.

## Insight Quality

A useful insight is specific, evidence-based, actionable, and proportionate to the evidence. Quantify only with a known numerator, denominator, and source. A single observation may be important, but it is not a general pattern without corroboration.

Rank findings by decision impact and confidence. Lead with the few findings that materially change scope, priority, design, architecture, or rollout. State what would reduce uncertainty for any high-impact, low-confidence conclusion.

## Decision Brief Shape

Use this outline when the user has not requested a different format:

```markdown
## Decision
<question, scope, and decision owner if known>

## Findings
<theme, evidence, confidence, and implication>

## Contradictions And Limits
<conflicting evidence, scope limits, and assumptions>

## Options And Recommendation
<tradeoffs, recommended next action, and why>

## Open Questions
<what remains unknown and how it could be resolved>
```
