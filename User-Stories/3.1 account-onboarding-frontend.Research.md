# Research — Guided Account Onboarding

## Why This Story Exists

The first user interaction with a resume platform is not neutral. If onboarding is too heavy, users abandon before they experience builder value. If onboarding is too vague, they mistrust pricing and data handling. The selected competitors both reduce first-use friction, but they do so through slightly different product shapes. That gives us a useful, grounded basis for a developer-ready requirement.

## Why Novorésumé And Resume Now Were Chosen

- Novorésumé provides a simpler and more transparent entry model for MVP inspiration.
- Resume Now provides clearer evidence of guided progression and post-signup account continuity.
- Together they cover both "fast to start" and "guided to complete" onboarding approaches.
- Zety remains useful later for breadth analysis, but it introduces more product-surface noise for kickoff requirements.

## Competitive Decomposition

### Novorésumé Signals

- Start-free positioning indicates low-friction account creation.
- No-credit-card-first messaging lowers commitment anxiety.
- Builder-entry flow suggests product value is demonstrated quickly after authentication.
- Public template and AI-tool cross-links imply onboarding is closely tied to downstream creation journeys.

### Resume Now Signals

- Guided questions imply onboarding is not just identity capture but also psychological guidance into resume-building.
- Support content implies a strong need for recoverable auth and saved-work continuity.
- Pricing plus FAQ suggest that the account exists as a gateway to stored documents, downloads, and add-on tools.

## Observed User Problems This Story Must Solve

- Visitors abandon when forced through long registration before seeing product value.
- Visitors lose momentum when a selected template or chosen action is not preserved after signup.
- Users mistrust the product if they cannot tell whether registration is free or leads into paid flow.
- Users become confused when signup, login, verification, and builder start are presented as unrelated screens.

## Product Interpretation

- The product should start with a minimal registration flow.
- Users should reach resume creation within the first minute.
- Onboarding should capture only what is needed to create a session and initial workspace.
- Onboarding must preserve user intent from whichever source screen triggered signup.

## Screen-Level Requirement Inference

| Screen | Purpose | Required Elements | Classification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage CTA State | Convert visitor into starter | CTA, proof, free-start message | Observed / Recommended |
| Template CTA State | Convert template interest into signup or builder | template identity, CTA, deferred auth | Observed / Inferred |
| Register Screen / Modal | Capture account minimum | identity fields, password, consent, links | Inferred / Recommended |
| Verification Reminder | Encourage trust and account hygiene | status, resend, dismiss | Inferred / Recommended |
| Bootstrap Transition | Move user into product safely | loading, retry, route decision | Inferred / Recommended |

## Hidden Platform Requirements Inferred

- Immediate session creation or chained login after registration.
- Consent storage with legal-version traceability.
- Source attribution persistence through auth and redirect.
- Starter-workspace routing logic.
- Email verification status model.
- Partial-success handling when account creation succeeds but secondary initialization does not.

## Requirement Implications

- Frontend needs a short, resilient onboarding path.
- Backend needs consent-aware account creation and session issuance.
- QA should cover abandoned signup, duplicate email, partial success, and mobile completion.
- Analytics must capture which entry surfaces convert to actual builder starts.

## Developer-Facing Interpretation

- Treat onboarding as part of the creation funnel, not as a standalone utility page.
- Assume users may enter from multiple public routes and expect continuity.
- Avoid adding profile fields that do not directly help account creation or immediate product entry.
- Implement a deliberate partial-success state because it is realistic in real systems.

## Risks If Under-Specified

- Engineering may build a generic register screen that discards selected-template intent.
- Verification could accidentally block first-use value even if product wants soft enforcement.
- Bootstrap could become a fragile chain of follow-up calls instead of a stable entry contract.
- Analytics could miss the relationship between public CTA source and successful resume creation.

## Recommended Scope For MVP

- Email/password signup.
- Soft verification reminder.
- Source-aware redirect.
- Legal consent capture with auditability.
- No anonymous draft mode unless product explicitly asks for it.

## Open Questions

- Should MVP allow anonymous draft creation before signup?
- Should email verification block editing or only exporting?
- Should template selection happen before signup, after signup, or both?
- Should marketing consent behavior vary by region at launch?
