# Research — Content Inventory, Metadata, And Publication State Services

## Research Basis

The public acquisition behavior of the selected competitors strongly implies internal systems for managing templates, examples, and guidance assets. Even if the content-editing tools are not public, the scale and structure of those public surfaces indicate a content inventory and publication-management need.

## Requirement Interpretation

- Structured content inventory is required for public growth surfaces, because both competitors maintain large, filterable catalogs that could not stay current without a managed backend.
- Metadata and state management should be editable by authorized non-engineering operators, a pattern common in modern headless CMS architectures where content teams own catalog hygiene.
- Publication-state changes should be controlled and traceable, reflecting the industry norm of separating draft, published, and archived states behind explicit transitions.

## Risks If Under-Specified

- Engineering becomes a bottleneck for content catalog maintenance, forcing developers to make routine metadata changes that operators should own.
- Public-site metadata drifts from real product capability, so what the marketing surface promises and what the product delivers diverge.
- Premium/public flags become inconsistent, creating confusing or misleading acquisition experiences that hurt conversion and support.

## Recommended Backend Interpretation

- Build content inventory and metadata APIs with safe edit scope and auditability, establishing the catalog foundation other admin domains build on.
- Keep richer article/CMS editing out of MVP if needed, but support catalog operations early, since inventory control has the clearest immediate value.
