# Research — Template Administration And Publication Management Interface

## Why This Story Exists

Templates are one of the most visible assets in the selected competitor ecosystems. They affect acquisition, activation, premium conversion, and perceived product quality. Because templates are so central, a deeper dedicated administration surface is warranted rather than relying only on generic content-management stories.

## Business Interpretation

- Templates are a growth asset and a product-rendering dependency, sitting at the intersection of marketing acquisition and actual document generation quality.
- Premium/free assignment and public discoverability are commercial choices requiring admin support, since both competitors gate and position their catalogs deliberately.
- Template operations deserve domain-specific tooling beyond generic content rows, because template commercial state is richer than ordinary content tagging.

## Requirement Implications

- The admin UI should expose commercial and rendering-relevant metadata, so operators can see premium, ATS, style, and publication signals together.
- Template types and families should be modeled explicitly, since resume and cover-letter templates follow distinct lifecycle and rendering paths.
- Publication and premium state changes should be controlled and auditable, given their direct impact on monetization and public discovery.

## Risks If Under-Specified

- Template catalog quality degrades operationally, as stale or mislabeled templates accumulate without a management surface.
- Public discovery and actual entitlements drift apart, so users see premium templates they cannot actually access, or vice versa.
- Teams lack a safe way to manage template portfolio decisions, forcing ad hoc changes that are hard to trace or roll back.

## Recommended Scope Interpretation

- Focus on inventory, state, and metadata first, since these unlock safe portfolio management without requiring a full authoring tool.
- Leave deep layout editing or authoring tools to later phases, once operational control of the existing catalog is stable.
