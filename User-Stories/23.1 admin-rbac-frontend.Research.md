# Research — Admin User, Role, And Access Management Interface

## Why This Story Exists

The public product surfaces show enough operational complexity to justify explicit admin and RBAC stories even though no public competitor admin console is visible. Account recovery, billing, templates, AI, analysis, support, and entitlement behavior all imply operational roles and permission boundaries behind the scenes.

## Business Interpretation

- Admin and RBAC are not optional if the product is to scale safely, since a career platform with billing, support, and privacy obligations cannot rely on shared or ad hoc privileged access.
- Public-facing trust depends on internal access control even if users never see it directly, because data breaches and support mistakes are often traced back to weak internal permissions.
- Role-based operations are essential for supportability, compliance, and safe manual intervention, mirroring the least-privilege practices standard across regulated SaaS platforms.

## Requirement Implications

- User stories must eventually cover internal operations, not only end-user flows, since the product's operational surface is as important as its public one.
- FE admin experiences should be secure, intentional, and role-aware, reflecting what an operator can actually do rather than a generic admin view.
- BE role/profile models need a corresponding operator-facing management surface, closing the loop between definitions and day-to-day assignment.

## Risks If Under-Specified

- Operational changes become manual, inconsistent, or risky, as teams improvise access without a governed interface.
- Sensitive support or billing interventions may lack proper controls, exposing user data or enabling destructive actions.
- The platform may scale functionally but not operationally, becoming hard to manage safely as users and operators grow.

## Recommended Scope Interpretation

- Start with users, roles, and access-profile management as the admin foundation, since every other admin domain depends on this base layer.
- Extend later into billing, content, feature flags, and support tooling, layering additional capabilities onto the same RBAC foundation.
