## What The Research Means

- Users need a final confidence checkpoint before external use — both competitors invest in pre-export review experiences: Resume Now explicitly labels proofread/check steps in their guided flow, and Novorésumé's product messaging centers on "what you design is what others see." This signals that users value a dedicated moment to verify document correctness before sending it into the world, rather than exporting from the builder and hoping for the best.
- Review should consolidate multiple quality signals into one practical decision surface — Resume Now's pricing page references both ATS checks and resume-check features as separate quality dimensions, suggesting that a good review experience aggregates these into one view rather than forcing the user to navigate between disconnected tools.
- Sharing, if introduced, must be privacy-explicit and clearly read-only — neither competitor offers a public share-link feature in their current product, but both emphasize user control over document visibility (Novorésumé's trusted preview, Resume Now's account-gated access). If we introduce sharing, we must exceed this baseline by making the read-only boundary unmistakably clear.

## Requirement Interpretation

- This is not a collaboration suite. It is a final-review and handoff experience — competitor evidence shows that resume builders focus on individual creation and export quality, not real-time multi-user editing. Resume Now and Novorésumé both optimize for the solo job-seeker workflow, and our review feature should follow the same pattern: a confidence checkpoint, not a Google-Docs-style sharing system.
- Share links should be optional and controlled, not automatically public — neither competitor auto-shares documents. Our default must be private, with sharing as an explicit opt-in action. The product's foundation BRs identified public share links as a "likely future capability," which means it should be designed as an extension, not a core assumption of the review flow.
- Review should bridge builder, ATS, and export signals into one concise workspace — Resume Now's product architecture separates ATS checking from the main builder, but our review page should unify these signals so the user doesn't context-switch. The value is in consolidation, not in adding yet another tool to the workflow.

## Risks If Under-Specified

- Final handoff remains fragmented across builder, ATS, and export screens — without a dedicated review page, users must mentally assemble readiness from disconnected tools. Resume Now bundles proofread/check into their guided flow, suggesting that fragmentation increases the likelihood users will skip quality checks entirely.
- Users may share sensitive documents without understanding visibility — if the share-link UI does not clearly communicate that "anyone with the link can view this document," users may inadvertently expose personal contact details, work history, or addresses. This is a higher-stakes issue for a resume product than for general document sharing because resume content is inherently personal and sensitive.
- Teams may overbuild comments/collaboration beyond current product scope — without explicit scoping, there is a risk that development drifts toward annotation features, threaded comments, or recruiter portals. Neither Resume Now nor Novorésumé offers real-time collaboration on resumes, and our initial scope should match the market norm: solo creation with optional view-only sharing.

## Recommended Scope Interpretation

- Focus on readiness summary plus optional read-only sharing — the research confirms that final-review is a validated need (both competitors invest in it) while sharing is an extension opportunity. The MVP should deliver a strong review page and treat share links as an optional, policy-gated feature that can be toggled on when the product is ready.
- Keep collaborative annotations and recruiter portals out of scope — neither competitor offers these features in their core resume builder, and adding them would shift the product toward a different category (collaboration platform vs. resume tool). The review feature should stay focused on the individual job seeker's confidence and control.
