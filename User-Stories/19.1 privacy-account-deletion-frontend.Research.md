# Research — Privacy, Data Export, And Account Deletion Self-Service Experience

## Why This Story Exists

Public privacy policies and support content from both selected competitors indicate that data rights, account deletion, and privacy-related support are not hidden legal-only concerns. In a product that stores resumes, cover letters, billing state, and potentially job descriptions, privacy self-service contributes directly to trust.

## Business Interpretation

- Users in this category care about what happens to sensitive career data.
- Self-service privacy actions can reduce support load while increasing trust.
- Deletion and retention messaging must be especially honest because user assets are valuable and personal.

## Requirement Implications

- Privacy actions need dedicated UX, not just buried legal links; users seeking data export or deletion should find these as first-class actions within the settings or account area, not as references in a long privacy policy document they must scroll through.
- Deletion consequences must be explained in plain language, including what happens to resumes, cover letters, billing history, and personal data — not in legal boilerplate that users skip or misunderstand.
- BE needs request-state handling and policy-aware messaging support so the frontend can show accurate next steps for each possible outcome (accepted, pending, blocked, requires escalation) without fabricating states from HTTP codes alone.

## Risks If Under-Specified

- Users feel the platform is hiding or complicating data rights.
- Privacy support load increases.
- Deletion flows become risky or ambiguous.

## Recommended Scope Interpretation

- Focus MVP on clarity, supportability, and safe request initiation.
- Keep complex back-office privacy-case handling out of the user-facing scope.
