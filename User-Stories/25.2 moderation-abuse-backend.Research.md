# Research — Moderation Case Retrieval And Abuse Review Services

## Research Basis

Public competitor product behavior implies trust and safety needs even when the internal tooling is not visible. AI assistance, uploads, account creation, and billing flows all create abuse and policy-review scenarios that require explicit operational handling.

## Requirement Interpretation

- Internal moderation case handling should exist as a trust/safety service, providing a governed API surface separate from general admin endpoints.
- Case categories and severity are necessary for meaningful review, giving reviewers the taxonomy they need to triage and route cases.
- Access must be carefully controlled and auditable, since moderation data is more sensitive than ordinary product administration.

## Risks If Under-Specified

- Abuse and policy issues remain unstructured and harder to resolve, as flagged activity lacks a consistent case representation.
- Engineering becomes the only path for many trust/safety investigations, slowing response and concentrating sensitive access in developers.
- Sensitive data may be handled inconsistently, raising privacy risk when different operators see different levels of detail without clear policy.

## Recommended Backend Interpretation

- Start with a review-case service and tightly scoped internal access, establishing a safe, curated baseline before adding complexity.
- Expand to richer detection/automation later, once the case lifecycle and access model are proven in production.
