# Research — AI Content Suggestions And Rewrite Controls

## Why This Story Exists

AI writing assistance has become a table-stakes expectation in resume products, not a novelty. Both selected competitors package AI as task-specific, reviewable assistance — not as an opaque "magic" box. This story must reflect that: AI that is scoped, honest, and controlled by the user.

## Competitive Interpretation

### Novorésumé Signals

- AI is packaged as a toolkit of named, discrete tasks — not one generic "AI" button. Users understand what they are invoking.
- The explicit "never invents qualifications" / "true to your experience" positioning is both a trust signal and a guardrail: AI assists, but does not fabricate credentials, metrics, or roles.
- "One free use per tool" gives users a genuine taste without gating the first interaction.
- AI output is editable like any other text after acceptance — it does not become a locked artifact.
- Credits/quota are visible ("2 left this month"), preventing "you ran out with no warning" frustration.

### Resume Now Signals

- AI is also broken into named capabilities (builder, summary, skills) rather than a single monolithic feature.
- Content improvement and template formatting are treated as separate concerns — AI helps with words; templates handle layout.

## Product Interpretation

- AI must be task-scoped, named, and reviewable, not an opaque single-button experience.
- The safety stance (no fabrication, always reviewable) is a differentiator if communicated well.
- A free-use model (one real result, not a blurred preview) is proven to drive upgrade without false scarcity.
- Quota visibility must be built into the UI from the start.
- AI actions should live in-context (inside the builder) rather than in a separate detached chatbot surface.

## Hidden Requirements Inferred

- Section-to-action mapping (which sections support which AI actions).
- Prompt engineering guardrails (never invent qualifications, metrics, or fake experience).
- Moderation layer (block toxic, inflated, or fabricated outputs).
- Version-linked suggestions (so stale AI content is flagged after resume data changes).
- Quota/entitlement tracking per action type, not just one global counter.
- Safe-handling for provider failure (timeout, rate limit, partial output).

## Scope Guidance

| AI Action | In MVP | Context needed | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Generate Summary | Yes | role, experience level, target industry | most-demanded by free users |
| Generate/Improve Bullets | Yes | role, existing text or target job desc | ties to tailoring |
| Rewrite Section | Yes | existing text, optional job desc | reviewable suggest/accept model |
| Generate Skills | Should Have | experience, target industry | from existing context or job desc |
| Generate Objective | Should Have | background, target role | especially for career changers |
| Tailor to Job Description | Should Have | job description text | highest-value premium AI action |
| Generate Cover Letter | Could Have (cover-letter domain) | resume data + job description | requires cover-letter domain |
| Bio / Salary / etc. | Could Have | user context | lower priority for resume platform |

## Risks If Under-Specified

- AI may be built as a detached chat interface that has no structural connection to resume sections.
- Without explicit no-fabrication guardrails, AI may generate invented titles, metrics, or credentials — damaging trust.
- Users may hit a quota wall without warning if credits are not surfaced in the UI.
- Free-vs-premium AI boundaries may be unclear, causing upgrade resentment.

## Recommendation To Developers

- Model AI actions as discrete, named capabilities with defined input contracts per section type.
- Keep AI output reviewable until explicitly accepted; never auto-replace user text.
- Build quota tracking and premium gating as first-class UI states, not retrofitted later.
- Design the suggestion review experience (compare before/after, accept/reject/re-try/dismiss) as its own UX component.

## Open Questions

- Which exact AI actions are launch-ready vs. phased?
- Is the one-free-use-per-tool model appropriate for our monetization, or do we prefer a credit system only?
- Should AI suggestions carry a "reviewed by human" badge in later phases?
- Do we limit AI by section, by action count, or by a global quota?
