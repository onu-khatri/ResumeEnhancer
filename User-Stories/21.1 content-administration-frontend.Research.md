# Research — Content Administration Interface For Templates, Examples, And Public Resources

## Why This Story Exists

Public competitor product strategy relies heavily on templates, examples, and guides as acquisition infrastructure. That implies a real internal content-management need even if public users never see the operations behind it. Without content administration, the public funnel becomes brittle and expensive to maintain manually.

## Business Interpretation

- Public content is a product-growth asset: in the resume-software market, template and example libraries are among the primary organic acquisition channels, so their freshness directly drives signups.
- Internal operations need controlled ways to manage status and categorization, mirroring how content-led SaaS businesses maintain catalogs through role-based CMS tooling rather than manual file edits.
- Template and example metadata directly influence acquisition and onboarding quality, since mismatched premium flags or stale publication states degrade the first-run experience and conversion.

## Requirement Implications

- Content admin should support at least metadata and publication-state management, because those are the operations most likely to unblock non-engineering teams in the near term.
- Full CMS richness can wait, but list/filter/state operations are important, since operators first need reliable inventory visibility before richer authoring tools matter.
- Content operations should be role-protected and auditable, following the same internal-control expectations that apply across the rest of the admin surface.

## Risks If Under-Specified

- Public assets become harder to maintain consistently, leading to a catalog that drifts out of date and underperforms as an acquisition channel.
- Premium/public mismatch can appear in acquisition surfaces, where a template shown as free is actually gated, eroding user trust and inflating support volume.
- Content teams depend on engineering for simple catalog changes, creating an avoidable bottleneck that slows response to market and content needs.

## Recommended Scope Interpretation

- Start with structured metadata operations for templates, examples, and public resources, which deliver the highest operational value for the lowest complexity.
- Expand to richer editorial tooling later if needed, once the inventory foundation is stable and the workflow is proven.
