# Research — Internal Analytics Summary And Audit Log Retrieval Services

## Research Basis

The selected competitors’ public operational complexity implies internal visibility requirements even though the tools themselves are not visible. Paid product surfaces, privacy obligations, support channels, and entitlement-sensitive flows all benefit from internal analytics and auditability.

## Requirement Interpretation

- Internal observability is part of operating a trustworthy product, since operators need governed access to metrics and audit events rather than raw database access.
- Audit and analytics should be treated as product operations capabilities, not only infrastructure outputs, so they are curated and permissioned like any other internal feature.
- Access to these views must be controlled and scoped, aligning with the least-privilege and audit-trail expectations common to compliance-conscious platforms.

## Risks If Under-Specified

- Sensitive operational changes become hard to investigate, leaving incidents without a reliable reconstruction of what happened.
- Support and compliance work becomes slower and more manual, as teams resort to ad hoc queries or engineering requests.
- Internal teams rely on ad hoc engineering support for routine questions, creating a bottleneck that observability tooling is meant to remove.

## Recommended Backend Interpretation

- Start with focused summary metrics and queryable audit logs, covering the highest-value operator questions without building a full warehouse.
- Keep the scope read-only and tightly permissioned in MVP, so the first iteration is safe and fast to ship.
