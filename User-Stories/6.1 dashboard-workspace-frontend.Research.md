# Research — Saved Documents Workspace And Resume Management

## Why This Story Exists

The current builder and import stories assume a user already has a route into editing, but repeat usage requires a durable workspace. Public competitor evidence shows that users are expected to manage multiple documents, revisit them later, and tailor copies over time. That means a dashboard/workspace is not a cosmetic afterthought; it is part of the product’s retention model.

## Business Interpretation

- Saved-document management is part of product value, not just account plumbing.
- The workspace is where retained users judge whether the platform supports real ongoing job-search behavior.
- A user who wants to tailor multiple applications needs quick document access and duplication.

## Requirement Implications

- The platform needs a durable document index model, not only direct editor routes.
- UX must support both zero-document and many-document states.
- BE must support lightweight document metadata retrieval distinct from full editor payloads.
- Novorésumé's "My Documents" pattern (Views: All/Resumes/Cover Letters; custom Tags for language/role/market) and its reusable "My Content" library are the proven models: user reviews repeatedly cite easy duplication/tailoring as a top reason to stay. The workspace should surface duplication and organization as primary actions, not buried settings.

## Risks If Under-Specified

- Users may struggle to find or differentiate saved documents.
- Tailoring behavior becomes cumbersome if duplication requires deep editor entry.
- Support teams will face more “where is my resume?” complaints.

## Recommended Scope Interpretation

- Treat this workspace as the primary authenticated home for repeat use.
- Optimize for re-entry, organization, and continuation.
- Keep document management simple but trustworthy in MVP.
