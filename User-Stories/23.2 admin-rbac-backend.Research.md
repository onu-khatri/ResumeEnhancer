# Research — Role, Access Profile, And Admin User Management Services

## Research Basis

Although competitor admin systems are not publicly visible, the public products clearly imply internal operators managing billing, support, privacy, data access, content, and account issues. That requires a structured admin/RBAC layer rather than ad hoc privileged access.

## Requirement Interpretation

- Admin/RBAC is an operational necessity even if users never see it directly, because every sensitive internal action ultimately depends on a governed permission model.
- Role/access definitions should be explicit, not hidden in application code only, so operators can inspect and reason about who can do what without reading source.
- Auditability is part of operational trust and compliance, aligning with the audit-logging expectations common to SOC 2 and privacy-conscious product organizations.

## Risks If Under-Specified

- Support and operations become overpowered or inconsistent, as broad admin roles grant more access than any single operator needs.
- Sensitive access changes may be untracked, making it impossible to reconstruct who changed a user's privileges during an incident.
- The platform becomes harder to scale safely, since hard-coded role checks cannot keep pace with a growing set of operational personas.

## Recommended Backend Interpretation

- Treat RBAC as a first-class platform service, centralizing role and access definitions rather than scattering checks across modules.
- Support user-role and access-profile modeling early rather than hard-coding every internal role forever, so the system can evolve without schema churn.
