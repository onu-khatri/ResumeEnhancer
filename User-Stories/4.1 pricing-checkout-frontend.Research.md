# Research — Pricing, Upgrade, And Checkout Continuity Experience

## Research Basis

Pricing in resume products is not only a marketing concern. Public competitor evidence shows it is part of product trust, because the purchase moment usually happens after a user has already invested effort in a resume. That makes pricing clarity, premium-lock explanation, and return continuity essential product requirements.

## What The Research Means

- Novorésumé uses pricing clarity as part of trust-building. Concrete signals: time-boxed pay-once Premium tiers (Month/Quarter/Year), explicit "no automatic subscription / no hidden costs" messaging, a **14-day money-back guarantee**, payments via Stripe and PayPal, and "data retained after Premium expiry" (access reduced, content kept). Pricing FAQ directly answers "Will my Premium account renew automatically? No."
- Novorésumé's "preview premium features, pay to download" (white-star) model is the core trust-preserving gate: users can test premium features free but must pay to download a document using them.
- Resume Now shows how aggressively monetization can be integrated into the flow (free plain-text vs. paid PDF/DOCX, recurring renewal), which increases the need for precise explanatory copy.
- Both references imply that the upgrade moment is strongest when tied to visible value such as export, template access, or advanced help.

## Requirement Interpretation

- Developers should treat pricing and checkout as a routed product journey, not a static marketing page.
- The upgrade surface must preserve origin context so the app can return the user to export, template selection, AI, or analysis after purchase.
- The frontend must not infer billing truth only from query-string success flags; it needs verified post-checkout state from the backend.

## Why This Story Matters

Without this story:

- plan differences become inconsistent across marketing and product;
- users may return from checkout and lose the task that motivated them to pay;
- support and refund pressure increases because users cannot tell whether they paid successfully or what they unlocked.

## Recommended Product Interpretation

- Favor transparent comparison over aggressive urgency tactics.
- Keep locked-feature explanations specific to the user’s current task.
- Treat post-checkout return as a first-class UX state with success, canceled, failed, and pending branches.
